# Prototype kit: the breakfast stall

The paper prototype for ticket 2. You are 老板, the owner of a small breakfast stall. Customers come up, say what they want, and you hand it over and take the money. There is no English anywhere in play, no pinyin, and you never have to speak. Audio for every customer line is in `prototype-counter/audio/`; the clip ids match the lines below.

This file is the **player kit**. Do not read [[prototype-counter-facilitator]] before playing; it has the answers.

## Rules of the stall

1. Nobody uses English, in speech or on paper. If you are stuck, the customer repeats, then points, then shows you. They never translate.
2. You never have to talk. Nod, point, hand things over, give change.
3. Replay a clip as often as you like. That is the one unlimited help.
4. Act before you check. Hand over what you think they asked for, take the money, give change, *then* look at the receipt key.
5. If you are wrong, the customer frowns (the facilitator or you, solo, note it) and you try again. No penalty beyond that.

## Before you play

Tick what you already know. This is the "known set". If you tick fewer than half of groups A to C, play the demo twice.

**A. Greetings and small words:** 你好 · 谢谢 · 老板 · 早 · 再见 · 好 · 对 · 不
**B. Numbers:** 一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九 十
**C. Ordering:** 来 · 要 · 个 · 多少钱 · 块
**D. On the menu:** 包子 · 馒头 · 鸡蛋 · 油条 · 豆浆 · 粥 · 饺子 · 茶
**E. Containers:** 杯 · 碗 · 根
**F. Hot, cold, big, small:** 热 · 冰 · 大 · 小
**G. Asking and adding:** 有…吗 · 没有 · 还要 · 这个 · 那个 · 那
**H. The regulars' word:** 老样子

Forty-five words. Groups D to H are what the stall is there to teach. You are not expected to know them.

## Materials

Make these on paper or index cards. Draw badly; it does not matter.

- **Item cards**, several of each, each with a small picture and the characters written on it (the stall labels its goods):
  - 包子 (round steamed bun with pleats on top), 馒头 (plain round bun, no pleats), 鸡蛋 (brown tea egg), 油条 (long fried dough stick), 饺子 (a bowl of crescent dumplings), 茶 (a cup of tea)
  - 豆浆 in two cups: 热豆浆 (cup with steam lines) and 冰豆浆 (cup with ice cubes)
  - 粥 in two bowls: 大碗 (big bowl) and 小碗 (small bowl)
- **The menu board**, written out and propped up where you can see it:

  | | |
  |---|---|
  | 包子 | 2块 / 个 |
  | 馒头 | 1块 / 个 |
  | 鸡蛋 | 2块 / 个 |
  | 油条 | 2块 / 根 |
  | 豆浆 (热 / 冰) | 3块 / 杯 |
  | 粥 (大碗 5块) | 小碗 3块 |
  | 饺子 | 8块 / 碗 |
  | 茶 | 3块 / 杯 |

- **Money**: coins or paper for 1块, 2块, 5块, 10块, 20块. Give the customer side a pile; keep a float of small change on your side.
- **Four regulars' portraits** (any doodle): 王奶奶, 小李, 小朋友, 张老师. Put them face down; turn one up when that regular visits.

## How a visit goes

1. A customer arrives. Play their clip (or the facilitator says the line). Read the characters at the same time; they are printed under each clip id below.
2. Hand over the items.
3. The customer pays (the amount is in the key). Give change.
4. Check the receipt key for that visit. Right: next customer. Wrong: replay, look at what they pointed at, try once more.

Solo: play the clip yourself and play both sides of the counter. The key tells you how much they paid.

## Phase 0: watch first (demo)

Watch two customers served by someone else (the facilitator, or just play the clips in order and move the cards yourself). Nothing is asked of you yet.

- `D1a` 老板，早！来两个包子。 → `D1b` 好，两个包子。 → `D1c` 多少钱？ → `D1d` 四块。 → `D1e` 谢谢！再见！
- `D2a` 你好！来一个包子。 → `D2b` 一个包子，两块。 → `D2c` 谢谢。
- `D3a` 老板，三个包子多少钱？ → `D3b` 六块。

## While you play, notice

Jot these down as they happen; they go into [[prototype-counter]] afterwards.

- Which line did you *not* get, and what would have helped (a repeat, a point, a picture, the sound again)?
- When did you want English? Write the exact moment.
- Did handing over the right thing feel like winning, or like passing a quiz?
- Did you start hearing 杯 / 碗 / 根 as "the thing that goes with that item" without being told?
- By 王奶奶's third visit, did 老样子 just mean "her bowl and bun"?
- Were the tones audible enough from the clips alone to tell words apart? Which pairs blurred?
- Was the rush fun, stressful, or both?

# AI-generated game assets in 2026: what is achievable for a solo, cozy, dialogue-heavy 2D game

Research question: what is the 2026 frontier for generating game assets with AI for a small team that wants a good-looking 2D game, and which art style is most achievable with consistency? Context: Junyan — a Chinese breakfast stall, later a small town; 4-10 recurring characters with portraits and expressions; item icons; simple UI; light animation; music/ambient; Mandarin TTS for hundreds of short lines. No in-house artist; assets generated by the founder and integrated by coding agents.

Caveat on sources: most 2026 "comparison" pages are vendor or SEO blogs. Where a claim rests only on such a page it is marked *(vendor)* or *(unverified)*. Primary docs were read where reachable (OpenAI, Gemini, Steamworks, MiniMax, Scenario, Stability, Suno, Spine, ElevenLabs).

## The 10 decision-relevant facts

1. **Character consistency is now an edit-in-place problem, not a prompt problem.** The current frontier models all take multiple reference images and edit an existing image while preserving identity: Gemini image models accept up to 4-5 character references and multi-turn editing via `previous_interaction_id` ([Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)); OpenAI's `gpt-image-2.5` takes multiple input images with masks and has a `background: "transparent"` option ([OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)); Midjourney V7 has Omni Reference (`--oref`/`--ow`) ([summary](https://www.cometapi.com/?p=25326), official docs returned 403). "Change only the facial expression, keep hair/outfit/framing" is the standard recipe for expression sets ([Ludo](https://ludo.ai/tools/character-portrait-generator), [QuestStudio](https://queststudio.io/blog/best-ai-models-for-character-consistency)). No model guarantees 1:1 identity; expect retakes.
2. **Illustrated (clean-line anime / painterly flat) is the lowest-cleanup style for AI; pixel art is the highest.** Generic models produce off-grid, anti-aliased, 200-colour "pixel art" that needs Aseprite work ([FreeGameSprites](https://freegamesprites.com/en/news/ai-pixel-art-generation-2026-tools-and-workflows), [Ludo](https://ludo.ai/compare/best-ai-pixel-art-generators)). Dedicated tools (Retro Diffusion, PixelLab) fix the grid but are their own ecosystem.
3. **Transparent cutouts are a solved API feature on OpenAI (`background: transparent`, PNG/WebP) and a product feature on Ludo/PixelLab; Gemini image models have no alpha output and need a background-removal pass** ([OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation), [apiyi FAQ](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/faq/image-transparent-background), [DEV test](https://dev.to/owen_fox/transparent-background-is-not-supported-for-this-model-3-fixes-5b1l)).
4. **"Static portrait + a handful of expressions + light rig" is exactly how the reference cozy games were made.** Coffee Talk's lead artist: 1-2 days of pixel art per character, then ~1 day in Spine for the animation ([Toge devlog](https://togeproductions.itch.io/coffee-talk-demo/devlog/66764/coffee-talk-devlog-2-bringing-doodles-to-life)); VA-11 Hall-A sprites are a body plus swappable eyes/lips layers ([TCRF](https://new.tcrf.net/VA-11_Hall-A:_Cyberpunk_Bartender_Action)). Reviewers note Coffee Talk's "very limited animation" didn't hurt ([Unwinnable](https://unwinnable.com/?p=78740)).
5. **Mandarin TTS: Fish Audio S2 Pro, MiniMax Speech 2.8 and (self-hosted) CosyVoice 3 / Qwen3-TTS are the Mandarin leaders; ElevenLabs is the emotion leader but weaker on Chinese polyphones.** Fish's own blind test gives Chinese BT scores Fish S2 Pro 8.11 vs ElevenLabs v3 2.36 ([Fish, vendor-run](https://fish.audio/blog/blind-tts-provider-comparison-2026/)); a Taiwanese-accent re-test puts MiniMax first on naturalness ([Medium](https://medium.com/@colaflyfly/three-years-later-re-testing-the-chinese-tts-tools-with-taiwanese-accent-ea4980df4dbb)); a Chinese-language buyer's guide puts ElevenLabs v3, MiniMax 2.8 and Qwen-TTS/CosyVoice 3 in the top tier ([peihuachen guide](https://peihuachen.github.io/ai-tools-hub/posts/tts-commercial-open-source-comparison-guide-2026.html)).
6. **Tone correctness is controllable, not just hoped for.** MiniMax's T2A API takes a pronunciation dictionary and inline pinyin overrides with tone numbers (`处理/(chu3)(li3)`), plus an `emotion` enum and `language_boost: Chinese` ([MiniMax docs](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/api-reference/speech-t2a-http)). For a language-learning game this is a decisive feature.
7. **TTS cost is a rounding error at this scale.** Hundreds of short lines ≈ 10-50k characters. MiniMax 2.8-hd $100/M chars, turbo $60/M; Fish S2 Pro $15/M bytes (≈$45/M Chinese chars); ElevenLabs v3 ~$100/M list; OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts ~$12-15/M effective ([invideo Aug 2026](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-voice-model-pricing/), [texttolab](https://texttolab.com/blog/fish-audio-pricing)). Even the priciest option is a few dollars for the whole script; spend on quality.
8. **Steam requires disclosure of any player-facing AI content, but treats pre-generated AI art "the same way we evaluate all non-AI content".** Dev-time efficiency tools are explicitly "not the focus" ([Steamworks content survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)). Apple has no generative-art disclosure rule (1.2 covers AI *creation* apps; 5.1.2(i) covers sharing personal data with third-party AI) ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google Play's gen-AI policy scopes to apps that *generate* content at runtime ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294)); third-party blogs claim a 2026 self-declaration for AI-made store assets *(unverified)*.
9. **Licensing traps are in open-weight models, not hosted APIs.** FLUX.1/2-dev weights are non-commercial (outputs are debated) ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/flux-ai-image-generator/), [flowith](https://flowith.io/blog/flux-2-pro-dev-faq-licensing-lora-fine-tuning-api-rate-limits-self-hosting/)); Illustrious XL is FAIPL-1.0-SD, which discourages closed-source monetisation of the model ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/OnomaAIResearch/Illustrious-xl-early-release-v0)); Hunyuan3D's licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanImage-2.1/discussions/3)); Fish S2 weights are research-only ([ocdevel](https://ocdevel.com/blog/20250720-tts)). Hosted APIs (OpenAI, Gemini, Scenario paid, Suno Pro, ElevenLabs, MiniMax) grant commercial use of outputs.
10. **Agent-driven pipelines exist today and are thin wrappers: a Claude Code skill calling an image API, then rembg/trim/pack.** PixelLab ships a CLI that installs a Claude Code skill ([pypi](https://pypi.org/project/pixellab-cli/)); community skills chain nano-banana-pro + background removal on fal ([GitHub](https://github.com/adamlyttleapps/claude-skill-game-assets-enhancer)); ComfyUI headless with a Python wrapper is the local equivalent ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/comfyui-game-asset-pipeline-indie-2026)).

## 1. Image generation for 2D game art in 2026

**Frontier hosted models.** Three families dominate practitioner writeups:

- **Google Gemini image ("Nano Banana" family).** Current names per the official docs: `gemini-nano-banana-2.1` (recommended), `gemini-3.1-flash-image`, `gemini-3-pro-image` (premium), `gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image`, legacy `gemini-2.5-flash-image`. Up to 14 mixed reference images per request; 4 character references (5 on Pro); 3 style references (Pro only); multi-turn editing via `previous_interaction_id`; 1K/2K/4K output ([Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). Pricing (third-party read of Google's page, Sep 2026): Nano Banana 2 $0.067/1K image, $0.101/2K; Pro $0.134/1K-2K, $0.24/4K ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/media-pricing/nano-banana)) *(verify)*. Repeatedly rated strongest for multi-reference scene fusion and iterative edits ([thedaringcreatives](https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/ai-image-consistency/), [floatboat](https://floatboat.ai/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-midjourney-nano-banana-2)). No alpha channel output.
- **OpenAI `gpt-image-2.5`** (`-sunburst` for editing precision, `-flare` for speed). Multi-image input with mask on the first image; `background: "transparent"` with PNG/WebP ([OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Rated best for high-fidelity edits and step-by-step chat editing, with a caveat that drift accumulates across repeated edits ([QuestStudio](https://queststudio.io/blog/best-ai-models-for-character-consistency)).
- **Midjourney V7 Omni Reference** (`--oref`, `--ow` 0-1000, default 100; ~2x GPU cost; V7 only) ([CometAPI](https://www.cometapi.com/?p=25326), [Lilys](https://lilys.ai/notes/en/ai-midjourney-20251111/make-consistent-midjourney-characters)). Strongest *style* control; weakest API story (no official API for individuals) and style can mask identity drift. Common pattern: concept in Midjourney, then turnaround/expressions in Nano Banana ([midlibrary](https://midlibrary.io/midguide/midjourney-vs-nano-banana)).
- **FLUX 2** takes up to 8 reference images via API ([QuestStudio](https://queststudio.io/blog/best-ai-models-for-character-consistency)); the open `-dev` weights are non-commercial, the 4B Klein is Apache-2.0 ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/flux-ai-image-generator/)).

**Game-specific platforms.**

- **Scenario** — train a style LoRA from 10-30 images or a character from 5-15; Starter $15/mo, Pro $45/mo (includes custom model training), Max $75/mo; "you own what you create", paid plans carry a commercial licence, free outputs are evaluation-only ([Scenario pricing](https://www.scenario.com/pricing)). Has ControlNet-style pose control, Unity/Photoshop plugins and a late-2025 pixel-art mode ([TT Lab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-ai-game-asset-generation-npc-2026-scenario-layer-promethean-charmed-inworld-convai-trellis-meshy-deep-dive?lang=en)). Repeatedly described as the answer to style drift across hundreds of assets ([3D AI Studio](https://www.3daistudio.com/3d-generator-ai-comparison-alternatives-guide/can-i-use-ai-for-indie-game-assets)).
- **Layer.ai** — "coherent bundle" generation (sheet of idle/walk/jump keyframes, 20 icons in one palette); positioned at studios doing LiveOps volume; pricing not public ([TT Lab](https://ttlab.hopto.org/blog/culture/2026-05-16-ai-game-asset-generation-npc-2026-scenario-layer-promethean-charmed-inworld-convai-trellis-meshy-deep-dive?lang=en)).
- **Leonardo** — cheap, has consistent-character mode and fine-tuning; reviewers rate face consistency as "good but not production-ready" and dropping in full-body shots ([toonystory, vendor](https://toonystory.com/blog/best-ai-for-character-consistency-2026)).
- **Ludo.ai** — portrait generator aimed at dialogue games: style-locked cast, expressions via "change only the facial expression" edits, cutouts with no background, animated-portrait sprite sheets (blink/breathe/talk, up to 64 frames, loop mode) ([Ludo](https://ludo.ai/tools/character-portrait-generator)). Worth a trial precisely because it packages the workflow Junyan needs.
- **PixelLab** (pixel art): characters, 4/8-direction rotations, skeleton animation, tilesets, UI, inpainting, API + MCP server; ~$10-50/mo tiers, commercial use on paid tiers *(third-party)* ([pixellab.ai](https://www.pixellab.ai/), [knowara](https://knowara.com/ai-tools/image/pixellab-review/)).
- **Retro Diffusion** (pixel art): grid-aligned, palette-limited models; Aseprite extension $65 one-time (Lite $20); licence shipped inside the extension ([itch](https://astropulse.itch.io/retrodiffusion)); free "pixel-art-fixer" for off-grid output ([GitHub](https://github.com/Retro-Diffusion/pixel-art-fixer/wiki)).

**Consistency across expressions and poses.** The accepted protocol (converging across QuestStudio, Daring Creatives, Selfielab): one canonical reference per character → a character sheet (front/three-quarter/full body + expression row) generated as one image so the model keeps identity within a single canvas → every later asset is an *edit* of that sheet with the sheet attached as reference → never switch tools mid-series. QuestStudio's scoring rubric (face 40%, hair/features 20%, proportions 15%, outfit 10%, style 10%, artifacts 5%; pass at ≥85) is a useful acceptance gate for an agent to apply ([QuestStudio](https://queststudio.io/blog/best-ai-models-for-character-consistency)). In the one 140-image test found, Midjourney `--cref` scored 7.5/10 and Flux+PuLID 6.5 on identity; the test predates Omni Reference and Nano Banana Pro and was run by a vendor that ranked itself first ([toonystory](https://toonystory.com/blog/best-ai-for-character-consistency-2026)).

**Palette consistency across a set.** Two working approaches: (a) a style reference image (Gemini Pro style refs, Scenario style LoRA, Midjourney `--sref`) on every call; (b) post-hoc palette quantisation to a fixed project palette (trivial in a script for flat/illustrated art; mandatory anyway for pixel art). The "scene brief" pattern — one paragraph of setting/time-of-day/palette/mood prepended to every prompt — is what the open-source Gemini outpainting tool uses to keep parallax/tiles/props aligned ([image-extender](https://github.com/boona13/image-extender/wiki)).

## 2. Which style is cleanest from AI?

- **Pixel art**: generic models break the grid, anti-alias edges, and inflate palettes; frames across a sheet "look like they come from different games"; cleanup "can take longer than drawing from scratch" ([FreeGameSprites](https://freegamesprites.com/en/news/ai-pixel-art-generation-2026-tools-and-workflows)). Dedicated tools fix grid/palette but an itch.io practitioner says AI pixel art in 2026 "mostly works as placeholder" needing manual pixel fixes ([itch thread](https://itch.io/t/7084124/ive-made-3-games-using-ai-heres-what-ive-learned-so-far)). Pixel art also makes AI-ness *more* visible to the pixel-art community, who are the most hostile audience ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/it-creates-lesser-games-indie-game-studios-are-supposed-to-love-generative-ai-so-why-do-the-25-devs-i-spoke-to-avoid-it/)).
- **Illustrated 2D (anime-adjacent clean line + flat shading)**: the native output of every frontier model and of Illustrious-class open models; edits-in-place work best here because there is no grid to respect. Downside: the most recognisable "AI look" and "oversaturated, looks alike" complaint ([itch thread](https://itch.io/t/7084124/ive-made-3-games-using-ai-heres-what-ive-learned-so-far)); mitigated by a deliberate, slightly unusual style spec (limited palette, visible line weight, textured paper) and a style LoRA.
- **Painterly**: beautiful single images, worst for consistency across a set and worst for cutouts (soft edges). Fine for backgrounds, bad for sprites.
- **Flat vector / "Kurzgesagt" style**: very consistent, cutouts are trivial, but models output raster not SVG; shading is simple enough that palette quantisation yields near-perfect cohesion. Good candidate for icons and UI.

Practitioner consensus: AI is "excellent at one image at a time"; animation and tiling are where it struggles ([SummerEngine](https://www.summerengine.com/blog/ai-2d-game-asset-generator)). So pick the style where each asset *is* one image: illustrated portraits and painted/flat backgrounds.

## 3. Animation

- **The cozy-game shortcut is real.** Coffee Talk: pixel art 1-2 days + Spine ~1 day per character; reviewers praise personality "despite the very limited animation" ([devlog](https://togeproductions.itch.io/coffee-talk-demo/devlog/66764/coffee-talk-devlog-2-bringing-doodles-to-life), [Unwinnable](https://unwinnable.com/?p=78740)). VA-11 Hall-A: body + eyes + lips layers ([TCRF](https://new.tcrf.net/VA-11_Hall-A:_Cyberpunk_Bartender_Action)). A VN dev's planning method: read the whole script, thumbnail an expression per line, dedupe ([itch blog](https://itch.io/blog/762711/how-i-created-thousands-of-facial-expressions)). For Junyan: 5-7 expressions per character, swapped mouth layer for talking, 2-3 px bob for idle, hand-over as a 3-frame arm swap.
- **Cheapest tools.** Spine Essential $69 / Pro $379 per seat, Enterprise above $500k revenue ([Spine](https://esotericsoftware.com/spine-purchase)). Live2D Cubism PRO for indie applies under ¥10M revenue ([Live2D help](https://help.live2d.com/en/?p=2172)); auto-rigging a single AI image into Live2D is still a hobby pipeline, "no production-level automation" ([img2rig](https://github.com/SSnemo/img2rig), [blog](https://waifuai.blogspot.com/2025/12/building-automated-image-to-live2d.html)). Simplest of all: Godot AnimationPlayer tweens on layered PNGs — no rigging tool at all.
- **AI motion.** Ludo animated portraits (blink/breathe/talk loops → sprite sheet) ([Ludo](https://ludo.ai/tools/character-portrait-generator)); PixelLab skeleton/text-prompt animation for pixel sprites ([pixellab.ai](https://www.pixellab.ai/)); image-to-video (Kling 3.0 / Wan 2.7) then frame extraction — vendor-only evidence and loops have a visible seam ([Sorceress, vendor](https://sorceress.games/blog/animate-an-ai-animation-picture-generator-image-to-loop)); Sora API discontinued, so don't build on one video API ([search](https://nerdbot.com/2026/10/20/top-10-ai-video-character-generators-for-creative-projects-in-2026/)). Talking-photo tools (HeyGen etc.) are tuned for photoreal faces ([Akool](https://akool.com/blog-posts/top-5-ai-talking-photo-generators-for-animated-video-creation)).

## 4. Backgrounds and tilesets

- **Scene-based, not tilemap.** For a point-and-click/dialogue game, one large background sprite per location with hotspot nodes is the normal Godot pattern; tilemaps buy nothing when there is no traversal ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/tile-based-interaction-how-to-structure-the-game/29261)). Watch the 4096px texture limit on web exports ([r/godot](https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/godot/comments/1f4bvbs/importing_handdrawn_open_world_maps/lkkig0p/?context=3)).
- **Time-of-day variants.** Generate the base scene once, then *edit* it ("same composition, dawn light, lanterns on") rather than re-prompting; hand-made parallax packs keep silhouettes identical across day/sunset/night so layers can be swapped — the constraint to copy ([kibbitz devlog](https://kibbitz.itch.io/parallax-backgrounds-24-seamless-scenes-138-layers-8-biomes-x-day-sunset-night/devlog/1724767/parallax-backgrounds-24-seamless-scenes-138-layers-8-biomes-x-day-sunset-night)). Cheaper still: one daytime painting plus a colour-grade overlay/shader for dawn/evening, which guarantees consistency.
- **Parallax layers.** Generate the full scene, then segment into sky / far street / stall / foreground props with background removal and outpaint the gaps (Poisson-blended outpainting pipeline in [image-extender](https://github.com/boona13/image-extender/wiki)). 2-3 layers is plenty for a cozy stall.
- **Tilesets** only if the town later needs free walking; PixelLab/Scenario produce tilesets but seams need manual work ([SummerEngine](https://www.summerengine.com/blog/ai-2d-game-asset-generator)).

## 5. 3D as an alternative

April 2026 same-prompt test: Tripo P1 best on stylised/cartoon, Meshy 5 "slightly realistic when cartoon was requested", Rodin Gen-2 highest fidelity but "stylistically wrong for cartoon"; CSM has since been acquired and shut down ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/generative-3d-tools-comparison-meshy-rodin-tripo-csm-2026)). Pricing: Meshy Pro $20/mo (free tier requires CC-BY), Tripo $24-99, Rodin $29-149; API ≈$0.10-0.30/model ([3D AI Studio](https://www.3daistudio.com/blog/best-3d-model-generation-apis-2026)). Hunyuan3D is open but its licence excludes EU/UK/Korea ([HF](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanImage-2.1/discussions/3)).

Verdict for a diorama look: harder, not easier, to make cohesive with AI. Every mesh needs import/scale/collision/retopo, textures drift per-asset, and generators bias toward realism; one reviewer estimated ~40 hours cleanup on a two-month Meshy project ([inker](https://inker.si/blog/best-ai-3d-model-generators-2026)). Unpacking and A Short Hike get cohesion from hand-authored palettes and a single artist's eye, which is exactly what AI 3D lacks. 3D only becomes attractive if the game needs camera movement or many angles of the same object — Junyan doesn't.

## 6. Audio

**Music.** ElevenLabs Music and Stable Audio 3 are the licence-clean choices (licensed training data; Stable Audio under the Community License, free below $1M revenue) ([gudz](https://gudz.ai/posts/ai-music-generation-2026), [Stability licence](https://stability.ai/license)). Suno: free-tier songs are owned by Suno and non-commercial; Pro/Premier grant ownership, though Suno warns purely AI output may not be copyrightable ([Suno help](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2746945)); litigation status unsettled. Udio has no downloads ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-music-generation-platforms-suno-udio-elevenlabs-2026)). For a Chinese-stall soundscape, Stable Audio 3 instrumental beds + a few guzheng/erhu loops is a defensible stack.

**SFX.** ElevenLabs SFX v2 (0.5-30 s clips; pricing inconsistent across sources, ~$0.002/s on fal) and Stable Audio 3 Small-SFX runs locally under the Community License ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-music-apis/elevenlabs-sfx-api/), [gudz](https://gudz.ai/posts/ai-music-generation-2026)). Sizzle, ladle, steam and street ambience are also abundant on freesound under CC0 — check each clip's licence.

Voice consistency per character: all hosted leaders support fixed voice IDs and instant cloning (MiniMax ~10 s sample, Seed-ICL ~5 s). Practical plan: pick 1-2 candidate providers, render the same 20 lines (including known polyphones: 行, 得, 了, 还, 重, 长; and tone-sandhi cases: 不是, 一个, 你好) through each, and have a native speaker blind-rank. Keep every line's pinyin in the script so tone overrides are machine-applicable.

## 7. Legal / licensing posture

- **Steam**: content survey requires describing any AI used for content players consume (art, sound, narrative, localisation); "efficiency gains... not the focus"; pre-generated content is reviewed like non-AI content; live-generated content additionally requires describing guardrails; live-generated AO sexual content is refused ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)). Secondary reporting says a January 2026 form rewrite split this into a store-page tag (pre-generated) vs a dedicated "AI Content" section (live), with coding assistants/MCP/build tools exempt, and advises specific over vague wording ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/steam-ai-disclosure-rules-2026-indie-developer-guide)) *(secondary)*. ~1 in 5 listings now carry the label *(unverified)* ([tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026/)). Epic has no equivalent rule.
- **Apple**: no rule on AI-made art; 1.2 safeguards apply to apps where users generate content; 5.1.2(i) requires disclosure + consent before personal data goes to third-party AI (relevant only if you ship runtime LLM/TTS calls with user speech) ([Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- **Google Play**: gen-AI policy targets apps that generate content; pre-generated art not addressed; blogs claim in-app labelling (from Apr 2026) and a store-asset self-declaration ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294), [deesu](https://deesu.org/blog/google-play-ai-labeling-asset-declaration-requirements)) *(unverified)*.
- **Models**: see fact 9. Scenario paid plans and OpenAI/Gemini API outputs are commercially usable; Meshy free tier is CC-BY; Suno free tier is non-commercial; FLUX-dev weights non-commercial; Illustrious FAIPL; Hunyuan3D geo-restricted; Fish S2 weights research-only.
- **How indies handle it**: disclose specifically ("character portraits and backgrounds were generated with X and hand-edited; voices are synthetic"); expect a vocal minority to refuse on principle ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/it-creates-lesser-games-indie-game-studios-are-supposed-to-love-generative-ai-so-why-do-the-25-devs-i-spoke-to-avoid-it/), [itch](https://itch.io/t/7084124/ive-made-3-games-using-ai-heres-what-ive-learned-so-far)); at least one studio swapped AI key art for hand-made after backlash ([itch](https://itch.io/post/15081252)). The audience for a Mandarin-learning game is less art-purist than the pixel-art indie crowd, which lowers this risk.

## 8. Pipelines

The pattern across StraySpark, Atlas, Scenario and the agent skills:

1. **Style bible** — one page: palette (hex list), line weight, lighting, materials, "never" list, 3-5 anchor images. Reused verbatim as the prompt prefix ("scene brief") ([Atlas](https://atlas.design/workflows/consistent-ai-art-style), [image-extender](https://github.com/boona13/image-extender/wiki)).
2. **Reference sheets** — per character: turnaround + expression row as a single image; per location: base scene at noon.
3. **Prompt templates** — `{style_bible} + {character_sheet ref} + "change only {delta}"`; keep aspect ratio and framing constants.
4. **Batch + review** — generate N candidates, score with a rubric (QuestStudio's), keep 1; closed tools randomise seeds so log everything ([StraySpark ComfyUI](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/comfyui-game-asset-pipeline-indie-2026)).
5. **Post** — background removal (OpenAI alpha output, or rembg/pixelcut for Gemini), trim, palette quantise, downscale, atlas pack (TexturePacker/free-tex-packer), write a manifest JSON the game reads.
6. **Agent glue** — PixelLab CLI installs a Claude Code skill and recipe ([pypi](https://pypi.org/project/pixellab-cli/)); community skills do nano-banana-pro → rembg on fal ([GitHub](https://github.com/adamlyttleapps/claude-skill-game-assets-enhancer)); ComfyUI headless + Python wrapper for local ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/comfyui-game-asset-pipeline-indie-2026)). For Junyan a 200-line script calling the Gemini/OpenAI image API with the style bible and writing to `assets/` with a manifest is enough; the engine never sees prompts.

Local vs hosted: local ComfyUI wins on reproducibility and cost at volume (RTX 3060 12 GB ≈ $400 runs it) but needs LoRA training discipline for style lock ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/comfyui-game-asset-pipeline-indie-2026)); for ~300 total assets hosted APIs are cheaper in founder-time.

## Recommendation for Junyan

**Style**: clean-line illustrated 2D with flat/cel shading and a fixed 24-32 colour palette — "modern anime-adjacent but with a hand-drawn ink line and paper texture" — not pixel art, not painterly. Portraits head-and-shoulders on transparent; backgrounds as full painted scenes at 2K in the same palette; icons as flat, outlined, palette-quantised cutouts.

**Toolchain**:
- Characters: Nano Banana (Pro for sheets, 2.1 for edits) or gpt-image-2.5-sunburst; one sheet per character, expressions by edit; transparent via OpenAI `background` or rembg. Trial Ludo for its portrait+expression+animated-portrait bundle. Optionally lock style with a Scenario Pro LoRA ($45/mo) once 20 approved images exist.
- Backgrounds: same model, base scene then edited dawn/evening variants or a shader grade; 2-3 parallax layers via segmentation + outpaint.
- Animation: layered PNGs (body, eyes, mouth, arm) animated in Godot; Spine Essential ($69) only if needed.
- Audio: Stable Audio 3 / ElevenLabs Music for beds; ElevenLabs SFX or freesound CC0.
- TTS: MiniMax Speech 2.8-hd as default (tone dictionary + emotion enum), Fish S2 Pro as the A/B contender; CosyVoice 3 as the self-hosted fallback. Budget < $20 for the whole script.
- Pipeline: a Claude-Code-driven script with a style bible, manifest, rubric gate and rembg/pack steps.
- Legal: disclose on Steam as pre-generated (art, voices); keep only hosted-API or paid-plan outputs; avoid FLUX-dev/Illustrious weights and Hunyuan3D for shipped content.

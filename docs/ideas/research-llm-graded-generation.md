# LLM-driven vocabulary-constrained generation — technical state of the art

Research question: can LLMs generate in-game French/Mandarin text constrained to a player's known vocabulary plus a controlled sliver of new words, reliably and cheaply? Scope: shipped LLM-NPC games, controlled/readability-constrained generation, graded-reader generation, known-word modelling, speech, cost/latency, and existing "LLM language-learning game" projects. Items marked **[unverified]** come from secondary sources or could not be confirmed against a primary page.

## Summary: the facts that most affect feasibility and architecture

1. **Prompting alone does not hold a vocabulary whitelist, but a verify-then-rewrite loop gets close to zero violations.** In [SRS-Stories (arXiv 2512.18362)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18362), Llama 3.1 70B with simple prompting produced 0.64% out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words at English B1; a plan-then-write strategy plus up to five "here are the unknown words, replace them" rewrite passes got to **0.01%**. A deterministic, non-LLM checker (normalise, tokenise, lemmatise, diff against the list) is the load-bearing component, not the model.
2. **Constrained decoding against an allowed-token set works mechanically but produced worse text than prompt+rewrite.** The same paper's constrained-beam-search baseline had **6.71% OOV** and grammar 3.22/5 versus ~4.0/5 for prompting. Token-level masking is cheap ([llguidance computes a mask in ~50µs/token for a 128k vocab](https://pypi.org/project/llguidance/)) but "allowed tokens" is not "allowed words": subword and character tokens let the model assemble words outside the list, and a hard mask can push it into ungrammatical corners.
3. **Level control via prompt is a frontier-model capability; small open models need finetuning.** [From Tarzan to Tolkien (ACL 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03030): GPT-4 best-prompt ControlError 0.28, Llama2-7B 1.53, Mistral-7B 1.19; a finetuned+PPO Llama2-7B ("CaLM") reached 0.39 at an estimated ≥25× lower compute cost than GPT-4.
4. **A system prompt's level constraint drifts over a multi-turn dialogue.** [Alignment drift in CEFR-prompted LLMs (arXiv 2505.08351)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08351v2) found 7–12B models' readability "slowly decrease[s] over time, reducing the differences between CEFR levels" across 9-turn Spanish tutoring dialogues; "prompting alone is too brittle for sustained, long-term interactional contexts". Verification must be per-utterance.
5. **Chinese grading is a tooling problem before it is a model problem.** Word segmentation is unreliable on learner-graded lists, so [hsk30](https://pypi.org/project/hsk30/) grades at character level (level at which cumulative character coverage hits 95%); SRS-Stories also counted Chinese OOV by character. There are *two* "HSK 3.0" documents (2021 national standard vs 2025 exam syllabus) that disagree on **41.5% of shared vocabulary**; regrading 102 published graded readers flipped **48%** of them. Pick one standard and store it with every graded artifact.
6. **French has free, high-quality graded lexical resources.** [FLELex / CEFRLex (UCLouvain)](https://cental.uclouvain.be/cefrlex) gives per-word frequency distributions across CEFR levels from a 777k-word corpus of FFL textbooks and readers, open-licensed, machine-readable. François & Fairon's CEFR-compliant readability models exist for French ([ITEC/AI readability formula](https://core.ac.uk/works/20262117)).
7. **The simplest credible known-word model is manual marking + FSRS.** On the [open SRS benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark) (≈350M Anki reviews), FSRS-7 log loss 0.337 vs Duolingo-style half-life regression 0.469 (AUC 0.72 vs 0.64). FSRS is open-source and needs only per-word review history; BKT has no forgetting mechanism and DKT does not reliably beat simpler models.
8. **Per-turn cost with small hosted models is well under a cent, even with a 3–6× verify/rewrite multiplier.** [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing): gpt-4o-mini $0.15/$0.60 per M tokens, gpt-5-nano $0.05/$0.40, gpt-4.1-nano $0.10/$0.40. A 1.5k-token prompt + 150-token reply ≈ $0.0003 on gpt-4o-mini; ×5 retries ≈ $0.0015. Local 7–8B Q4 models run ~40–50 tok/s on an RTX 3060 (secondary benchmarks, **[unverified]**).
9. **Shipped LLM-NPC games show a consistent pattern: strong virality, novelty decay, and reliability complaints.** Vaudeville sits at Steam "Mixed" with reviewers saying the novelty "wears off quickly" and NPCs contradict themselves ([Game Developer pre-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vaudeville-pre-mortem)); AI Dungeon hit ~$200k/month in API+infra at peak ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/03/13/chatgpt-and-generative-ai-are-booming-but-at-a-very-expensive-price.html)); Stanford's 25-agent Smallville cost "thousands of dollars in token credits" for two simulated days on gpt-3.5-turbo ([paper §8.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)). The 2025–26 trend is small on-device models for *behaviour* (inZOI Smart Zoi, 0.5B-param Minitron) and cached/pre-generated text.
10. **Speech is feasible for an indie, with caveats.** TTS: OpenAI tts-1 $15/M chars, Inworld TTS-2 Flash $5–15/M, Azure Neural ~$16–22/M **[secondary]**, open-source CosyVoice for native Mandarin prosody. STT: $0.003–0.006/min (gpt-4o-mini-transcribe, Whisper, Deepgram). Azure Pronunciation Assessment supports French and Mandarin. But *learner* speech — especially Mandarin tones — is under-studied for Whisper-class models; expect to evaluate yourself.
11. **No existing project has shipped the exact thing.** Academic LLM language games (GenQuest, Game Master LLM) are prompt-only, pilot-scale (n=9, n=14), and self-report level mismatch. Commercial "AI stories at your level" (Readlang Story Bot, Beelinguapp, LingQ simplify) use level labels, not the user's known-word set, and users report A1/intermediate requests coming back C2.

## 1. LLM-driven NPCs in shipped or notable games

**Platforms.** Inworld AI (founded 2021 for game NPCs) broadened through 2024–25 into a general voice/LLM runtime; its [current pricing](https://inworld.ai/pricing) is per-character TTS ($15–25/M chars on-demand, down to $5 enterprise), STT $0.10–0.15/hr, and hosted open LLMs from $0.07/M input tokens — no per-interaction NPC pricing any more. Convai claims sub-200ms first-token latency on its site, but developer forum threads report 5–20s to first speech with GPT-4o and better results with Llama-3-70B ([Convai forum](https://forum.convai.com/t/how-to-reduce-convai-grpc-response-latency/6018)); plan tiers quoted by third parties ($29–$1,199/month) conflict and the official pricing page did not render for me — **[unverified]**. NVIDIA ACE moved to on-device small language models: inZOI's "Smart Zoi" runs a "Mistral NeMo Minitron" SLM (NVIDIA's page reads ".5B billion parameter", presumably 0.5B) exclusively on RTX GPUs to drive Zois' *actions, thoughts and daily schedules*, with free-text player input — not open dialogue ([NVIDIA GeForce news](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-naraka-bladepoint-inzoi-launch-this-month/)). Community posts put the Smart Zoi minimum at RTX 3060 8GB and recommend turning it off on base specs ([Game8](https://game8.co/games/inZOI/archives/504226)). This is the closest shipped analogue to "Sims with LLM agents", and it deliberately kept the LLM away from player-facing language.

**Research.** [Generative Agents (Park et al. 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442): 25 agents, gpt-3.5-turbo, two game-days; the limitations section says it cost "thousands of dollars in token credits and [took] multiple days". [Affordable Generative Agents (arXiv 2402.02053)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02053) reproduces the setup by replacing repeated LLM calls with learned policies and compressing social memory, arguing agents in fixed environments produce only finite behaviours — i.e. cache them.

**Games.** *Suck Up!* (Proxima, 2023, $16.99) — speech-driven persuasion of LLM NPCs; well received on YouTube; no public cost/model/guardrail figures found (podcasts with founder Ran Mo exist but I could not read transcripts — **[unverified]**). *Vaudeville* (2023, Early Access): Steam "Mixed" (255 reviews); Game8 40/100; developer acknowledges negative reviews about "the limitations of the AI" while a single YouTube playthrough got 2.4M views in eight days ([pre-mortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vaudeville-pre-mortem)). *1001 Nights* (Ada Eden): GPT-4 king whose spoken keywords materialise as items; the team later *removed* real-time scene generation after "underwhelming feedback" and added prompts/feedback to help players ([AIIDE-23 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12915), [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/how-book-of-infinity-1001-nights-uses-ai-to-let-players-tell-their-own-stories)). *Retail Mage* (Jam & Tea, Nov 2024, $4.99): ~79% positive on 44 Steam reviews, <20k owners estimated ([SteamSpy](https://www.steamspy.com/app/3224380)). *Dead Meat* (Meaning Machine): "author-led machine learning" interrogation; a 68-participant Bristol study reported 95% found it enjoyable; release status unclear ([Premortem](https://premortem.games/?p=4206)). *AI Dungeon* (Latitude): ~$200k/month at 2021 peak, moved to AI21 and open models, bills fell below $100k/month, added subscriptions ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/03/13/chatgpt-and-generative-ai-are-booming-but-at-a-very-expensive-price.html)). *Hex Judge* (solo dev, 2025–26): on-device Gemma 2 9B → Gemma 4 E4B; conversation start-up fell from ~45s to <1s on an RTX 2060; supports 12 languages ([HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/gemma-2-9b-gemma-4-e4b-local-llm-in-a-commercial-indie-game/179166)).

**Lessons.** (a) Open-ended chat is the part players tire of and the part that breaks; constrained roles (persuade a door-keeper, interrogate one suspect) hold up better. (b) Every successful team moved the LLM off the critical path: smaller/on-device models, cached responses, daily usage caps with cached fallback (an [itch.io dev](https://itch.io/post/12774112) describes exactly this). (c) Guardrails are persona + state + "secret preamble" prompts plus post-filters; nobody reports a reliable prompt-only guardrail.

## 2. Controlled / readability-constrained generation

**CEFR-level control.** [From Tarzan to Tolkien (Malik, Mayhew, Piech, Bicknell; Findings of ACL 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03030) defines ControlError = (predicted CEFR − target)² with a feature-based regressor (R² ≈ 0.8). Few-shot GPT-4 reaches 0.28; Llama2-7B and Mistral-7B stay at 1.2–1.9 even with 2,200-token prompts. Finetuning a 7B on GPT-4 outputs then PPO ("CaLM") reaches 0.39; sampling 3 and picking the best gives 0.15. Humans could distinguish difficulty differences down to roughly ControlError 0.25. PPO was "highly unstable". Takeaway: for a solo project, *distil* a frontier model's graded outputs into a small model, or just use a frontier-class small model (Haiku/mini/Flash) and verify.

[Controlling Language Difficulty in Dialogues with Linguistic Features (arXiv 2509.14545)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14545) trains on dialogues annotated with explicit readability, syntactic and lexical features (and a composite metric, Dilaprix), claiming more stable control than label prompting — details not extracted **[unverified]**. The Arabic study [Can LLMs Control Readability? (arXiv 2606.21981)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21981) builds topic vocab lists by prompting, then filters against a graded lexicon — the same "LLM proposes, lexicon disposes" pattern.

**Vocabulary-constrained generation.** [SRS-Stories (arXiv 2512.18362)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18362v1) is the closest prior art: English (CEFR-J lists), Chinese (HSK 3.0, levels 3/4/5 ≈ B1/B2/C1) and Polish (frequency approximation); Llama 3.1 70B, Qwen2.5 72B, Aya 23 35B, DeepSeek R1 70B via vLLM at temperature 0. Three generation strategies (simple prompt; plan→outline→write; example-sentences-first) × three repair strategies (rewrite with list of unknowns; rewrite with unknowns highlighted; get synonyms then rewrite), each repair iterated ≤5 times ("flattens out after 5"). English B1 OOV: CBS 6.71%, simple 0.64%, examples-first 0.46%, planning 0.01%. Chinese character-OOV with simple prompting: HSK3 2.77%, HSK4 1.73%, HSK5 0.65%; but the synonym/highlight repair variants made Chinese *worse* (5–10%) while raising target-word coverage from 19% to ~79%. Human eval: prompting+repair beat CBS on grammar (4.08 vs 3.56, p=0.015) and on "use of learned words" (3.88 vs 1.24). LLM-judge correlations with humans were only moderate for English (r=0.56) and weaker for Chinese. A CogSci 2026 study notes that under strict vocabulary constraints both humans and constrained LMs over-use semantically light words ("do", "thing", "people") ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt02m6x923/qt02m6x923.pdf)) — expect blandness and plan for it in evaluation.

**Constrained decoding tooling.** Grammar/regex-constrained decoding ([Outlines](https://huggingface.co/blog/vivien/llm-decoding-with-regex-constraints), [llguidance](https://pypi.org/project/llguidance/)) precomputes allowed next-tokens and sets other logits to −∞; llguidance reports ~50µs per token mask. In principle a whitelist can be compiled to a regex of allowed words and used as a hard constraint on a local model via llama.cpp/vLLM. Caveats: (i) tokens are subwords — a whitelist regex must be enforced at the string level, which these libraries do, but Chinese needs character-sequence rules since there is no whitespace; (ii) classic lexically-constrained decoding ([NeuroLogic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12884), grid beam search / dynamic beam allocation) handles positive "must include" constraints well and negative constraints only via CNF tricks, and SRS-Stories shows the quality cost; (iii) hosted APIs do not expose arbitrary logit masks (OpenAI's `logit_bias` is limited to ~300 tokens), so hard masking implies self-hosting. Practical consensus from the above: **generate with a good model → check with a deterministic grader → targeted rewrite → fall back to pre-approved content after N retries**, and reserve hard masking for a local model if rewrite loops prove too costly.

**Chinese-specific.** [LLM4CGDS (Eng. Appl. of AI, 2026)](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/llm4cgds-large-language-model-based-agents-for-chinese-graded-doc/): rule-guided LLM agents with HSK 3.0 readability constraints and retrieval, no finetuning, beating direct prompting on readability control and meaning preservation (bands 1–3/4–6/7–9). [HSKBenchmark (arXiv 2511.15574)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15574) targets HSK 3–6 writing with 6.76M tokens of textbooks and a linguistically grounded evaluator. The [hsk30](https://pypi.org/project/hsk30/) author's "WriteToLevel" benchmark reports *human* writers hit an explicit HSK target only **61.8%** of the time — a useful ceiling-reality check. Segmentation: HSK word lists and segmenters disagree (phrase tokens absent from lists), so grade characters, or grade words with a dictionary-constrained segmenter seeded from the learner's own known-word list.

**French-specific.** No French-specific generation paper found; the general pattern transfers, and FLELex gives per-word CEFR frequency profiles for the grader. The main French-specific wrinkle is inflection: lemmatise before whitelist checks (spaCy `fr_core_news_md` is the usual choice; SRS-Stories lemmatised English and Polish for the same reason).

## 3. Graded-reader generation

**Products.** [LingQ](https://www.lingq.com/en/news/lingq-launches-ai-lessons/) offers one-click OpenAI-powered simplification of any lesson and Whisper transcription of imports; it adapts *level*, not the user's known-word set. [Readlang Story Bot](https://blog.readlang.com/2024/12/04/context-aware-translations-and-two-other-features.html) (GPT-4o, Premium $6/month) generates up to 1,000-word stories by genre/topic; a forum user requesting "intermediate Spanish" got C2-rated output and another asked for stories built from their personal word list — not implemented ([forum](https://forum.readlang.com/t/feature-request-ai-generated-text-from-recently-looked-up-words-and-phrases/1680)). [Beelinguapp "Create Stories"](https://beelinguapp.com/blog/ai-and-beelinguapp:-crafting-the-perfect-stories-for-language-learning) lets users pick length/difficulty/vocab; a 2025 review says the A1 setting reads as intermediate ([Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/beelinguapp-review/)). [Mandarin Mosaic's article generator](https://mandarinmosaic.com/tools/article-generator) is the only product I found that explicitly *generates, scores against an HSK rubric, and refines until it matches* — the architecture this project needs. Du Chinese and Jiveworld are human-authored with native audio (no AI generation).

**Difficulty assessment.** Chinese: CRIE (82 linguistic features; CRIE-CFL hits 75% exact / 99.6% adjacent CEFR level on 1,578 teacher-rated texts — [NTNU](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/crie-an-automated-analyzer-for-chinese-texts-2/)); Cathoven and studycli HSK analyzers (studycli defines level as the minimum HSK level giving ~95% vocabulary coverage); Chinese Text Analyser (desktop, known-word tracking, per-text "% understood" — 2014-era, status **[unverified]**). French: Kandel-Moles/Henry formulas are Flesch adaptations; François & Fairon's SVM over 46 features and a later deep-learning CEFR classifier for FFL ([UCLouvain](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/bitstreams/cebc5a91-029e-4c4f-b61b-1830a5b93447/download)). For this project the right primitive is not a readability score but **coverage against the player's own list** (tokens known / tokens total, plus count of distinct unknown lemmas), with a frequency-list prior (FLELex, HSK) for words the player has not yet met.

**Coverage theory.** Hu & Nation (2000) set 98% as "desirable" and 95% as minimal; Nation (2006) estimated 4,000 word families for 95% and 8–9,000 for 98% coverage of novels; Schmitt, Jiang & Grabe (2011) found no sharp threshold, only a steady curve ([Cambridge replication note](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/how-much-vocabulary-is-needed-to-use-english-replication-of-van-zeeland-schmitt-2012-nation-2006-and-cobb-2007/1D217A56A2E0056E67802A6A8360FDDE)). Because game text is short and context-rich (objects on screen, NPC gestures), the game can probably run nearer 95% than prose readers can — testable.

## 4. Known-word modelling

- **Manual marking** (LingQ "known/LingQ", Chinese Text Analyser, Migaku's unknown/learning/known/ignored/tracking statuses, with "known" ≈ review interval ≥21 days — [Migaku](https://migaku.com/starter-guide)) is the industry default and is what powers their comprehension-percentage overlays. Formulas are unpublished.
- **jpdb** schedules to the minute with what the community believes is an ML model trained on jpdb data; it ranks media by % of words known. Official algorithm description not found **[unverified]**.
- **Half-life regression** ([Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174/)): p = 2^(−Δ/h), h learned from word features + history; 45% error reduction over baselines, +12% activity in A/B; code and 13M-trace dataset released.
- **FSRS** dominates HLR on the [SRS benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark) (log loss 0.337 vs 0.469; HLR degrades badly, 0.705, when same-day reviews are included). Neural RWKV variants do better still (0.277) but are not worth it for a solo project.
- **Knowledge tracing**: BKT is a 2-state HMM with no forgetting; a Bayesian-IRT comparison found IRT variants match or beat DKT ([paper](https://paperswithcode.com/paper/back-to-the-basics-bayesian-extensions-of-irt)). Not suited to a vocabulary-sized item set with decay.

**Simplest credible model**: per-lemma FSRS state (stability, difficulty, last-seen) updated by in-game *exposures* (seen in dialogue, clicked for gloss = lapse, used correctly in a response = success); treat a word as "known" when predicted recall ≥ ~0.9; seed new players with a frequency-ordered list and a short placement check. The generator's whitelist is then {p(recall) ≥ 0.9} and the "sliver" is the FSRS due queue plus the next frequency-band words.

## 5. Speech

**TTS.** [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing): tts-1 $15/M chars, tts-1-hd $30/M, gpt-4o-mini-tts token-billed ($12/M audio-out tokens). [Inworld](https://inworld.ai/pricing): TTS-2 $25/M, TTS-2 Flash $15/M on-demand, ~1,000 chars/min. Azure Neural ~$16/M and Neural HD ~$22/M, Google Chirp 3 HD ~$30/M, ElevenLabs ~$50–100/M depending on model — all from third-party roundups **[unverified]**; Azure's own page rendered placeholders. Open-source: Kokoro (82M params, Apache-2, lists French and Mandarin, but a Mandarin-focused reviewer calls its Chinese "added, not native prosody" and recommends CosyVoice 2 for tones — [imlearningmandarin test bench](https://imlearningmandarin.com/gym/voice-lab/), opinion not benchmark); Fish Speech S2 claims top blind-test rank **[vendor claim]**. A game's dialogue volume is small: 10k lines × 60 chars = 600k chars ≈ $9 on tts-1. Pre-render and ship audio; TTS cost is a non-issue.

**STT / pronunciation.** gpt-4o-mini-transcribe $0.003/min, Whisper and gpt-4o-transcribe $0.006/min, Deepgram Nova ~$0.0043–0.0048/min. Azure Pronunciation Assessment (accuracy, fluency, prosody, completeness) covers French and Mandarin; priced per audio hour as a STT add-on (community says ≈$1/hr, **[unverified]**; 5 free hours/month on F0). Learner speech is the gap: Whisper studies on L2 French-speakers' English show the medium model "converges with what a native speaker understands" while small models produce plausible-looking learner errors ([Ballier et al.](https://www.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-04547597v1)); a WER study on a French L2 corpus exists but figures were not in reach ([SLTC](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/sltc/article/view/1043)); fine-tuning whisper-small on learner English cut WER 44% ([LearnerVoice](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.04280)). For Mandarin tone errors from learners I found only pre-LLM CALL work and no Whisper evaluation. Verdict: listening is cheap and solid; speaking input is feasible as *intent recognition* (did the player say roughly the right thing?) but tone/pronunciation scoring should be scoped as an experiment, probably on Azure's assessment API, not built in-house.

## 6. Cost and latency reality

- **Hosted small models**: $0.05–0.60 per M input tokens, $0.40–2.00 per M output ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); Gemini Flash-Lite ~$0.10, DeepSeek ~$0.14–0.28 per third-party roundups). A dialogue turn (1.5k in / 150 out) on gpt-4o-mini ≈ $0.0003; a grade-and-rewrite loop of 5 passes ≈ $0.0015; 200 turns per play-hour ≈ $0.06–0.30/hour at worst. Frontier models are 15–50× more; use them only for offline distillation/content generation.
- **Latency**: hosted small models give first token in ~0.3–1s; a full verify-rewrite loop adds 2–10s, which is too slow for live chat but fine for "the NPC turns to you" pre-fetch or for between-scene generation. Convai/Inworld field reports of 5–20s show what unmanaged pipelines feel like.
- **Local**: 7–8B Q4 models ≈ 40–50 tok/s on an RTX 3060, 50–75 on a 4070 (aggregated benchmarks, **[unverified]**); a 150-token reply in ~3–4s plus prompt processing. inZOI needs 8GB VRAM for a 0.5B model plus the game; a text-only game can afford more. Hex Judge's 45s→<1s start-up gain came from a smaller model, which hints at the real constraint: load time and VRAM contention with rendering.
- **Pre-generation / caching**: the dominant strategy in practice — cache by (NPC, intent, state) key, embedding-similarity reuse for paraphrases, daily LLM budget with cached fallback, and generating the next session's content between sessions ([abratabia notes](https://abratabia.com/llm-npcs/latency-and-cost.php), [itch.io dev](https://itch.io/post/12774112), Affordable Generative Agents). For a graded-vocabulary game this is doubly attractive: the whitelist changes slowly (daily), so a nightly batch can generate and *fully verify* the next day's dialogue trees, and only free-text player input needs live generation.

## 7. Explicitly "LLM-powered language-learning game" projects

- [GenQuest (arXiv 2510.04498)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04498v1): Claude 3.7 Sonnet text adventure, six openings at A1–C2 chosen by the player, GPT-4o glossary; 9 Chinese EFL undergraduates, five sessions, post-test only (mean 13.4/20); participants reported rare vocabulary in "higher levels", branch inconsistencies, >1 minute waits; authors suggest "lexical curation or frequency-based filters" as future work — i.e. what this project would build.
- [Game Master LLM (arXiv 2511.15504)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15504): GPT-4o game master for spoken slang practice; n=14 between-subjects; better immediate post-test and engagement than an AI-led classroom.
- [SRS-Stories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18362) (above) is the only project combining SRS-known vocabulary with generation and the only one covering Chinese.
- [DIY-MKG (arXiv 2507.01872)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01872), [WordCraft (arXiv 2602.00762)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00762), [CEFR-annotated WordNet (arXiv 2510.18466)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18466) are adjacent tooling (knowledge graphs, mnemonics, graded sense inventories).
- Commercial/indie: no Steam or itch.io title found that gates NPC language by the player's known vocabulary. Doki Doki AI Interrogation and Hex Judge are LLM-dialogue games in multiple languages but not pedagogical. A 30-person dissertation study found players preferred LLM dialogue over scripted reactive dialogue ([ISCTE](https://repositorio-iul.iscte.pt/handle/10071/36959)) — but that is preference at first contact, not retention.

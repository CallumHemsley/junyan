# Platform choice for Junyan v1

Question: for a cozy, dialogue-heavy 2D Mandarin-learning game with an LLM backend (breakfast stall, small town), should v1 be a web app, a mobile app, or a desktop/Steam game?

Items marked **[unverified]** come from secondary sources or could not be confirmed against a primary page. All numbers are as of Oct 2026 unless stated.

## Top decision-relevant facts

1. **Steam is where language games have actually sold.** Third-party estimates: Wagotabi (Aug 2025, Japanese RPG) ~40-80K copies, ~$350-590K gross ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/2701720/Wagotabi_A_Japanese_Journey), [GameRevenueData](https://gamerevenuedata.pages.dev/games/wagotabi-a-japanese-journey/)); Shashingo ~13-30K copies, ~$160-300K ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1632490/Shashingo_Learn_Japanese_with_Photography), [Datahumble](https://datahumble.com/shashingo-learn-japanese-with-photography-1632490)); Chants of Sennaar ~700K Steam copies, ~$11M gross [unverified, single tracker] ([GameSensor](https://gamesensor.info/app/1931770)). Wagotabi also ships on iOS/Android ([itch profile](https://itch.io/profile/wagotabi)).
2. **But the median Steam release earns almost nothing**: estimates range from ~$249 (2025 cohort, third-party) to ~$5K lifetime gross over all games ([VG Insights](https://wp.vginsights.com/?p=24), [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/median-steam-game-2026)). Wishlist conversion median is ~0.10-0.15x first week for >$10 games ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)).
3. **Mobile is where language-learning money is, but through app stores**: Duolingo's FY2025 10-K: 62% of revenue via Apple, 20% via Google; Stripe (web) processed ~12% in FY2024 ([10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm), [10-K FY2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208825000042/duol-20241231.htm)). Paid conversion ~9% of MAU.
4. **Web-first is a dev/distribution choice, not a revenue choice.** Industry Idle's dev: web was the preferred dev platform and "makes very little money"; Windows/Steam was ~99% of desktop players; iOS ~30% of mobile players but ~50% of mobile income ([ruoyusun.com](https://ruoyusun.com/2023/10/12/one-game-six-platforms.html)).
5. **Web-wrapped games are routinely accepted on Steam**: Cookie Clicker (Electron + Greenworks, 500+ achievements, $4.99) ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/1454400/info)), CrossCode (NW.js), Game Dev Tycoon (NW.js), Industry Idle (Electron), Vampire Survivors (Phaser, Electron pre-1.6) ([PCGamingWiki](https://gamecytus.netlify.app/host-https-www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Vampire_Survivors), [Phaser](https://phaser.io/news/2024/02/vampire-survivors-space-54)).
6. **Apple's 4.2 "Minimum Functionality" is the real wrapper risk on iOS**, not Steam. The guideline rejects apps that are "a repackaged website"; Capacitor apps with native plugins still get rejected ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Apple dev forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)). A game with real gameplay, offline handling and native audio is well clear of this.
7. **DOM text is the only renderer that gives CJK text + per-word tap targets for free.** Canvas engines rasterise text; bitmap fonts are explicitly discouraged for CJK by PixiJS docs ([PixiJS text docs](https://pixijs.download/release/docs/text.html)). `Intl.Segmenter` (word granularity, zh) is Baseline in all browsers since Firefox 125 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/intl-segmenter?hl=en)).
8. **Godot web export in 2026 defaults to single-threaded, which is the only mode that works reliably on iOS Safari**; native mobile exports "perform better by a significant margin"; no C# on web ([Godot 4.7 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). Unity 6 officially supports iOS Safari 15+ and Android Chrome 58+ on Web ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/webgl-browsercompatibility.html)).
9. **Hobby-scale backend is ~$0-30/month**: Supabase Free (500MB DB, 50K MAU) / Pro $25 ([supabase.com/pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing.md)); Cloudflare Workers Paid $5 min ([CF docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)); Fly shared-cpu-1x $1.94/mo ([fly.io/pricing](https://fly.io/pricing.md)). LLM + TTS per-player costs dominate, not hosting.
10. **Payments**: web = Stripe (~3%); App Store = 30% year one, 15% after a year or under the Small Business Program (<$1M) ([RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/small-business-program)); Google Play subscriptions 15% from day one. US storefront apps may now link out to external purchase ([Apple guideline 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), but the fee Apple can charge on those links is still in litigation ([Neon](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/the-latest-epic-v.-apple-appeal-ruling-december-2025-what-changed-for-game-developers), [AppleInsider Mar 2026](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/03/30/the-epic-vs-apple-case-wont-get-a-rehearing-for-app-store-fees)).

## 1. Reach and monetisation by platform

### Web (own domain / PWA, itch.io, Poki, CrazyGames)
- **Own domain**: zero platform cut (Stripe fees only), instant updates, no review. Discovery is entirely on you. Wordle went from 90 players (1 Nov 2021) to 300K in two months to millions daily, purely via share-text virality, and sold for "low seven figures" ([NPR/WHYY](https://whyy.org/npr-story/the-new-york-times-buys-wordle)). Dreaming Spanish is a web-first comprehensible-input product at $8/mo Premium with a later companion app ([palteca review](https://palteca.com/dreaming-spanish-review), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6453332575)); subscriber numbers are not public.
- **itch.io**: default 10% cut, adjustable 0-100% ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/4868546)). Good for demos/jam builds, near-zero revenue for most.
- **Poki / CrazyGames**: ad-revenue share. Poki: 100% on traffic you bring, 50/50 on traffic Poki brings; CrazyGames ~60% ad / 70% IAP [unverified, from a jam terms page] ([cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki), [CrazyGames guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-crazygames)). Poki's 2026 State of Web Gaming: average play session ~332 s, 37% play several times a day [unverified secondary] ([pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/consumer-tech/news/browser-gaming-is-heading-toward-3-billion-here-is-why-it-is-being-taken-seriously-76ac792e-a131-49db-b337-54232c57e6bf/)). Portal audiences skew young and casual; a ~5-minute session fits a "serve three customers" loop but not subscription monetisation. Note Poki/CrazyGames portal games cannot easily require accounts or sell subscriptions.

### iOS / Android
- Language learning is a subscription category. Price norms: Super Duolingo ~$12.99/mo or ~$84/yr ([duolingoguides](https://duolingoguides.com/how-much-is-super-duolingo/)); Speak $17.99/mo, $83.99/yr, no free tier ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speak-learn-english/id1286609883)).
- Conversion: Duolingo 12.2M paid subs on ~133M MAU (~9%) in Q4 2025 ([Duolingo Q3/Q4 coverage](https://quartr.com/events/duolingo-inc-duol-q3-2025_3P0xmPs3)). Category benchmarks for small apps: language learning ~2-5% install-to-paid on iOS [unverified] ([appsops](https://appsops.store/blog/language-learning-app-subscription-pricing-2026)); RevenueCat median 4.3% (low-priced) to 9.8% (high-priced) across categories ([RevenueCat 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). Education apps convert poorly compared to the app average ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/?p=95464)).
- Industry Idle data point: mobile "is a different business: it needs paid user acquisition" ([ruoyusun.com](https://ruoyusun.com/2023/10/12/one-game-six-platforms.html)).
- Fees: Apple 30% (15% under Small Business Program or after year one of a subscription); Google Play subscriptions 15% ([RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/small-business-program), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2020/11/18/apple-drops-app-store-fees/)).

### Desktop / Steam
- Steam cut 30% (25% after $10M), $100 fee recouped at $1K ([fungies](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained.md)).
- Comparable titles (third-party estimates, 30-50% error): Wagotabi 40-80K copies at ~$10-15; Shashingo 13-30K at $9.99-19.99; Chants of Sennaar ~700K [unverified]. Wagotabi hit 99% positive on 1,100+ reviews and peaked at only 281 concurrent players ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/2701720/charts)), i.e. a niche can sell well without big CCU.
- Wishlist maths: median 0.15x first-week sales per wishlist (>25K wishlists), 0.10x for >$10 games ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)); Zukowski finds 0.22 first-month sales per wishlist for 2023-25 releases ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/steam-wishlist-to-sales-study-shows-how-genre-release-timing-and-reviews-affect-success-nsfw-games-show-unusually-high-conversion-rate/)). 10K wishlists at launch -> ~1-2K first-week sales.
- Median outcome is bleak: ~$249-$5K lifetime gross depending on cohort/method ([VG Insights](https://wp.vginsights.com/?p=24), [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/median-steam-game-2026)). Steam is also a one-time-purchase market; an LLM-backed game has ongoing per-player inference cost, which a $15 one-off price must amortise (or you sell DLC/"season" content).

## 2. Technical fit (text-heavy, audio-heavy, CJK, per-word taps)

| Concern | Godot 4 web | Unity 6 Web | Phaser/PixiJS | React/DOM (+ canvas/Pixi for the scene) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CJK rendering | Engine rasterises via its own font stack; dynamic fonts work but you ship the TTF/OTF (Noto Sans SC is tens of MB unsubsetted) | TextMeshPro needs atlases; CJK atlases are large/dynamic | Canvas `Text` re-rasterises on every change; `BitmapText` skips glyphs missing from the atlas and CJK atlases can exceed GPU texture limits ([PixiJS docs](https://pixijs.download/release/docs/text.html)) | Browser text engine, subpixel AA, system CJK fonts as fallback, Google Fonts serves Noto Sans SC as ML-sliced `unicode-range` woff2 chunks so only used slices download ([font-range](https://www.github.com/black7375/font-range), [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/noto-sans-sc/cdn)) |
| Per-word tap in a dialogue line | Requires RichTextLabel meta tags or manual glyph hit-testing | Manual: TMP link tags + raycast | Manual: split into one Text object per word or hit-test glyph metrics | Free: one `<span>` per segment from `Intl.Segmenter('zh',{granularity:'word'})`, native touch targets, CSS ruby for pinyin, accessible |
| Audio on iOS Safari | Must unlock on gesture; Godot 4.3+ "Sample" playback gives low latency even single-threaded ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)) | Same gesture rule | Web Audio; same gesture rule | Web Audio / `<audio>`; same gesture rule. All four need a "tap to start" screen; iOS blocks Web Audio with ringer on silent [unverified] ([Matt Montag](https://www.mattmontag.com/web/unlock-web-audio-in-safari-for-ios-and-macos)) |
| Offline | PWA export caches after first load; browser may evict ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)) | PWA template | Service worker | Service worker + IndexedDB; same eviction caveats |
| Mid-range Android perf | WASM; docs say native export performs "better by a significant margin"; single-threaded default | Officially supported Chrome 58+ but heavy | Lightweight, 2D-only, good | Lightest; a 2D cozy scene in Pixi/canvas plus DOM UI is cheap |
| Bundle | ~tens of MB wasm+pck, gzip to ~1/4; itch.io does not gzip ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)) | 10-30 MB+ typical [unverified] | <1 MB engine | <1 MB + fonts (sliced) |
| Threads/SAB | Multithreaded needs COOP/COEP and breaks ads/third-party embeds; single-threaded is default and "works very well on macOS and iOS" | Similar | n/a | n/a |
| Language/tooling | GDScript (no C# on web) | C# | TS | TS |

Verdict: for a game whose core interaction is tapping words inside dialogue lines, DOM text wins decisively. The "scene" (stall, customers, town) can be plain DOM/CSS animation or a small Pixi canvas layered under DOM dialogue. Godot/Unity make sense only if the game turns into something with heavy real-time simulation, which a breakfast-stall dialogue game is not.

## 3. Cross-platform wrappers in 2026

- **Capacitor (web -> iOS/Android)**: official Phaser tutorial and Editor template exist ([Phaser + Capacitor](https://phaser.io/tutorials/bring-your-phaser-game-to-ios-and-android-with-capacitor)). Pitfalls: Apple 4.2 rejections for apps that look like a website ([Apple forum thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812889)); one tell reviewers use is airplane mode showing a browser error page ([MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/app-store-review-guidelines-webview-wrapper)). Android WebView suspends the AudioContext on background and doesn't resume it ([engineered.at postmortem](https://engineered.at/articles/cozy-space-survivors)). A Phaser roguelite dev went web -> Capacitor and found "extra polish" was the main cost ([Phaser interview](https://phaser.io/news/2026/07/fuel-field-phaser-roguelite-interview)). Apple's guideline 4.7 explicitly permits HTML5/JS mini-games inside apps ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- **Tauri 2 mobile**: uses WKWebView/Android System WebView; shipped mobile apps exist by 2025, but "no supported way to embed native UI alongside the webview" and real-device testing is essential ([Tauri PR](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/pull/14484), [tauri-games guide](https://abratabia.com/native-wrappers/tauri-games.php)). Less battle-tested than Capacitor for games.
- **Electron/Tauri on Steam**: accepted; see Cookie Clicker (Electron + Greenworks, Workshop, cloud saves, 500+ achievements) ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/1454400/info)), CrossCode (NW.js, SteamDB lists an NW.js tech page), Industry Idle (Electron, four different Steamworks bridges tried). Steamworks bridge: `steamworks.js` (MIT, Electron + NW.js), with `electronEnableSteamOverlay()`; Greenworks is "best-effort maintenance" ([steamworks.js](https://gittrend.io/repo/ceifa/steamworks.js)). Known pitfall: overlay sometimes fails on Windows because Steam treats the Electron app as "software-rasterized" (open issue) ([issue #102](https://www.Github.com/ceifa/steamworks.js/issues/102)); Linux/Steam Deck needed `--no-sandbox` and had the most platform-specific bugs; macOS notarization is slow and the audience <1% ([ruoyusun.com](https://ruoyusun.com/2023/10/12/one-game-six-platforms.html)).
- **Godot/Unity native mobile**: mature, and the native build sidesteps 4.2 entirely. Cost is giving up DOM text.
- **Expo + React Native Skia**: Skia runs on JSI/UI thread, Skia 2.0 needs New Architecture, web build pulls ~2.5 MB CanvasKit wasm ([pkgpulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/react-native-skia-vs-react-native-svg-vs-react-native-2026)). No published 2D-game benchmarks found; viable for a mostly-UI game but you lose the plain-browser dev loop. Not recommended for v1.

## 4. Backend implications

Workload: generate dialogue graded to the player's known-word set, TTS per line, per-player word model.

- **Online-required vs offline-first**: generation and TTS require the network anyway; the only thing that benefits from offline is replaying already-generated scenes and reviewing known words. Recommendation: online-required v1 with aggressive caching (pre-generate the next scene; cache TTS audio in IndexedDB/Cache Storage). Add offline-first later only if mobile retention demands it. Sync engines if needed: PowerSync (free tier + open edition, Supabase integration) ([PowerSync](https://www.powersync.com/blog/offline-first-apps-made-simple-supabase-powersync)); ElectricSQL (Postgres only). Realm/Atlas Device Sync EOL Sept 2025 [unverified].
- **Where player state lives**: Postgres (Supabase) as source of truth for the word model (word, exposures, last-seen, strength); client keeps a mirror for instant taps. Generated dialogue and TTS are cacheable content-addressed blobs (hash of line text + voice) in object storage/CDN, so repeated lines across players cost nothing.
- **Per-player cost drivers** (prices from primary docs where possible):
  - LLM: Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50 per MTok, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20 (Anthropic rates, Oct 2026). A scene of ~1.5K input (system + word list) and ~400 output tokens costs ~$0.0004 on Haiku, ~$0.007 on Sonnet. 20 scenes/day/player: Haiku ~$0.25/player/month, Sonnet ~$4/player/month. Vocabulary-constrained Mandarin may need Sonnet-class quality (see Ticket 3 spike).
  - TTS: Azure/Google neural ~$15-16 per 1M chars, standard ~$4 per 1M; Google free tier ~1M neural chars/month [unverified, vendor pages not fetched] ([apicostcalc](https://apicostcalc.com/text-to-speech-cost-calculator.html), [speechactors](https://speechactors.com/article/?p=3863)). ElevenLabs Flash ~$50/1M; MiniMax quoted $8-100/1M, sources conflict ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-voice-model-pricing/)). A 30-character line costs ~$0.0005 on neural; 300 lines/day/player ≈ $4.50/player/month uncached, far less with content-addressed caching since beginner vocabulary repeats heavily.
  - Hosting: Supabase Free (500MB DB, 50K MAU, pauses after a week idle) or Pro $25/mo ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing.md)); Cloudflare Workers Paid $5/mo min, D1 on free and paid, Durable Objects paid-only ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)); Fly shared-cpu-1x 256MB $1.94/mo ([Fly](https://fly.io/pricing.md)); Vercel Hobby free for a static front end.
- **Scale estimates** (order of magnitude): 100 players ≈ $0-30 hosting + $25-150 inference/TTS per month, i.e. a hobby bill. 10K players ≈ $25-100 hosting + $2.5K-15K inference/TTS per month depending on model tier and cache hit rate; at ~5% paid conversion that is 500 subscribers, so ~$5-30 ARPPU/month is needed just to cover inference. Inference cost per active player, not hosting, is the business risk.
- **Auth and payments friction**: web = Supabase Auth (magic link/OAuth) + Stripe, you keep ~97%. iOS/Android = must use IAP for digital content (3.1.1) unless US storefront link-out; Apple 30%/15%, Google 15%; US external links allowed but Apple's permitted commission unresolved ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Neon](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/the-latest-epic-v.-apple-appeal-ruling-december-2025-what-changed-for-game-developers)). Steam = Steam wallet, 30%, plus Steam OpenID is a convenient auth (Industry Idle used it to ban cheaters).

## 5. Precedents

- **Grew from web**: Wordle (web only, viral share text, 90 -> millions daily in ~2 months) ([WHYY](https://whyy.org/npr-story/the-new-york-times-buys-wordle)); Cookie Clicker (browser 2013 -> Steam 2021 as an Electron wrapper with achievements/Workshop) ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/cookie-clicker/steam), [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/1454400/info)); Vampire Survivors (Phaser browser build on itch -> Steam EA Dec 2021 -> re-engineered in Unity at 1.6 for consoles/mobile) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors), [Phaser](https://phaser.io/news/2024/02/vampire-survivors-space-54)); Industry Idle (web first-class, then Steam/iOS/Android) ([ruoyusun.com](https://ruoyusun.com/2023/10/12/one-game-six-platforms.html)); Dreaming Spanish (web video platform, app added later) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6453332575)). Duolingo is app-first: ~82% of revenue via app stores.
- **Regret / lessons**: Vampire Survivors left Phaser/Electron for Unity when it needed console and mobile ports (a cost of web-first at scale, not at v1). Industry Idle: Mac + Linux not worth it; mobile needs paid UA; "customer support, community management, cheater bans, and server maintenance took most of the author's time". A tactics game that used a web demo to drive Steam wishlists saw ~0.1% click-through ([r/gamedev postmortem](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/gamedev/comments/1khuhz2/tactics_game_postmortem_6_years_to_100k/mra6aeu)), so a web build is not a free wishlist machine.
- **Web-first-then-wrap writeups**: the Industry Idle six-platform article is the best primary writeup found; Phaser's Fuel Field interview (web -> Capacitor) and the cozy-space-survivors Capacitor audio postmortem cover the mobile step.

## 6. Developer loop for an agent-built project

- **Browser (DOM/Phaser) + Playwright**: headless Chromium, screenshots, console capture, DOM assertions and `Intl.Segmenter` all available with no custom tooling; deploy to Vercel/Cloudflare Pages on every push, so "ship daily" is a git push. Mobile checks via Playwright device emulation; real iOS Safari audio behaviour still needs a device or simulator.
- **Godot**: headless export works (`godot --headless --export-release "Web"`), but driving the game needs either PlayGodot (requires a custom Godot fork) or Chrome DevTools/Playwright against the canvas with no DOM to assert on ([PlayGodot/skills](https://playbooks.com/skills/chen19007/my-skills/godot-web-e2e), [Godot Claude Skills](https://github.com/Randroids-Dojo/Godot-Claude-Skills)). Screenshots are pixels only; text assertions need OCR or in-game debug hooks.
- **Unity**: editor-bound builds, slow iteration, Unity Test Framework; poor fit for a headless agent loop.
- **iOS simulator**: available in this environment, but adds a build step (Xcode) and review latency for anything shipped; use it as a weekly check, not the inner loop.
- **Desktop/Electron**: Playwright can drive Electron, but Steam builds add signing/notarization/depot upload; Industry Idle's notarization took "30 minutes to several hours".

## Recommendation

1. **v1: web app on your own domain** (React/DOM for dialogue and UI, optional Pixi/canvas or CSS for the stall scene), Supabase for auth + player word model, serverless API (Cloudflare Workers or Supabase Edge Functions) calling the LLM and a neural TTS with content-addressed caching. Online-required. Stripe if/when charging. PWA manifest so it installs to phone home screens.
2. **v2: Steam via Electron + steamworks.js** once a few hundred players retain, selling a one-off "season" with achievements; language games have a proven Steam niche and the wrapper precedent is strong. Build the Steam page early for wishlists regardless.
3. **v3: iOS/Android via Capacitor** only after Steam/web prove retention, because it is the platform with the most friction (4.2 review, IAP cut, Android WebView audio quirks) and the most UA dependence.

**Main risk**: per-player inference + TTS cost versus what a web/Steam audience will pay. A one-off Steam price does not match a recurring API bill; a web subscription matches it but web conversion for small language products is single-digit percent. Mitigate from day one with content-addressed TTS caching, pre-generated scene pools, and a cheap model tier for routine lines.

## C (orders)
Worst: 来一个饺子，不要冰 (one iced dumpling); 有老样子吗 ("the usual" as a product); 哪儿有饺子 (asks where dumplings are, at the stall); 您要冰吗 (vendor line with honorific); 十块一根，对不对 (absurd price invented to give 对不对 a target). One measure-word slip: 大碗粥，一个. Forced-word pattern: 冰 and 老样子 jammed into slots; question words drifting into vendor lines or nonsense; 请 / 早上好 / 好吗 / 等一下儿 textbook register.

## D (small talk)
Only 5 of 30 lines sound like a regular talking to a stall owner (我今天好累…来一碗粥; 饺子真的好吃，你吃了吗; 我要去上班，老板今天忙吗; 老板，我一会儿要去上班，粥好了吗; 我昨天吃了油条，今天还要吃). The rest: self-introductions the owner would already know (我教中文, 我是小学生), narration nobody needs (我昨天在小学吃了一个馒头), about a quarter are plain orders, lines addressed to someone other than the owner (小朋友…, 奶奶…), the vendor's question glued onto the order (我要一杯豆浆，要冰的吗), two unrelated clauses joined by a comma, forced words (中间, 吃冰), bare 妈 and 好吗 calques, 人太忙 for 人太多.

Structural finding: zero sentence-final particles (啊 呢 呀 嘛) in 30 lines, which alone makes everything stiff, and 呢 / 吧 were on the list. School words (教中文, 学院, 小学, 中学) recycled as filler in six lines: list vocabulary being used up rather than topics. Missing small-talk vocabulary the band-1 list does not have: 生意, 不错, 挺, 又, 刚, 下班, plus the particles.
